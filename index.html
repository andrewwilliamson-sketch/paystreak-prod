Option 02 · Worker cameras

Your crew's everyday work, earning every month.

The industry calls it egocentric data: video recorded from a worker's point of view with a small head- or chest-mounted camera. Companies teaching robots and AI to handle physical tasks need a steady supply of it, so it can become monthly income rather than a one-off sale.

  • Film the work you already do.

    Your team wears a lightweight camera on ordinary jobs. No staging and no scripts. The value is in real conditions, real tools, and real technique.

  • The buyer comes first.

    We agree what to film, how much, and the monthly rate with a buyer before anyone presses record, so your team never films anything nobody is paying for.

  • Consent on every frame.

    Everyone on camera agrees in writing, and customer homes and sites are recorded only with the owner's permission. What happens to the footage after delivery is set out in the license, and you approve it before anything goes out.

Most businesses fit one option. Some fit both.

You don't need to be technical, and you don't need to change how you work. The consultation works out which option fits, and which parts you can share and which you can't.

What it's worth

Five things set the price.

For data you already have, buyers weigh the same five factors every time. Your free consultation walks through each one.

  1. 01

    Size and volume

    How much documentation and history you have, and how many years it covers.

  2. 02

    Workflow complexity

    Multi-step processes with handoffs, decisions, and exceptions are worth more.

  3. 03

    Uniqueness

    Operational knowledge few other companies have written down.

  4. 04

    Data quality

    Current, consistent, and complete records that match how work really happens.

  5. 05

    Domain expertise

    The depth of specialist judgment your workflows capture.

Worker cameras are priced differently. Monthly income mostly depends on how many people record, how many hours of usable footage they produce, and how skilled the work is.

Fair questions

What a careful operator asks first.

What happens in the free consultation?

A short call about how your business works. We give you an overview of where the extra income could come from: which of your records and workflows have buyers, whether your team suits camera work, and roughly what each could be worth. No files change hands, and there's no obligation.

What's the catch?

There's no upfront cost, and the consultation is free. Paystreak earns an introduction fee only when a deal closes. Not every business finds a buyer, and some records can't be licensed at all. We'll tell you early which applies to you.

Is my company a fit?

If your team runs well-defined, documented workflows on modern software, licensing your records is likely a fit. If your team does skilled, hands-on work such as trades, field service, kitchens, warehouse, or manufacturing work, worker cameras are likely a fit. Some businesses fit both. If you're not sure, the free consultation will tell you.

Who wears the cameras, and how does the monthly income work?

Your workers wear a small head- or chest-mounted camera during their normal jobs. Before anyone records, we agree with a buyer what to film, how much, and the rate. Payment then comes in monthly for as long as your team keeps recording. Everyone on camera agrees in writing, and how you share the income with your team is up to you.

What about confidentiality?

How the data is handled is set out in the license between you and the buyer, and you approve what you share before anything goes out. Privileged legal communications, patient records, and similar material may need consent and legal review, and are often off the table entirely. We recommend having your own lawyer review any contract before you sign it.

Who are the buyers, and what do they do with the data?

Vetted programs that train and evaluate AI systems on realistic enterprise settings. They aren't trying to replicate your business, products, customers, or competitive advantages. Each buyer is vetted before we introduce them, and permitted uses are written into the license you approve.

What determines how much I can earn?

For data you already have, five things: the size and volume of the dataset, the complexity of the workflows it captures, how unique your operational knowledge is, the quality of the data, and the domain expertise it represents. For worker cameras, it mostly comes down to how many people record, how many hours of usable footage they produce, and how skilled the work is. Your free consultation covers both.

How does Paystreak get paid?

We earn an introduction fee when a deal closes. There's nothing to pay upfront, no retainer, and nothing at all if a deal doesn't close.
